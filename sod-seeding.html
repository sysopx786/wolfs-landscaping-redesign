Why aerate your lawn?

Aeration is one of the most useful things you can do to keep a lawn healthy and head off problems. Compacted soil and heavy thatch are the two biggest obstacles to a good-looking lawn, and aeration tackles both.

The small cores you see on the lawn afterward break down over the next week or two. That natural return to the soil helps keep thatch at a healthy level. It works best as a regular part of your lawn care, not a one-time fix.

  • Improves drainage
  • Gets more air, water and nutrients down to the roots
  • Relieves soil compaction
  • Stimulates new growth
  • Reduces thatch build-up
  • Helps water reach thirsty roots during dry spells
  • Creates a better bed for overseeding
  • Makes fertilizer work better

Spring and especially fall are the best times to aerate. We recommend doing it at least once a year. It's a relatively low-cost service that makes a real difference in how your turf looks.

Why overseed?

Overseeding fills in the thin areas where grass has died off. We can top dress, aerate and overseed your lawn so it comes back thicker, greener and healthier.

Your lawn is at its best for taking seed right after aeration, which is why the two jobs go together so well. It's a simple, affordable two-step process that can help even a stressed lawn, including one worn down by too much rain or a dry summer. Aerating and overseeding in the fall sets your Royersford and Chester County lawn up for a healthy summer next year.

After care: aeration and overseeding

Once your lawn has been aerated and overseeded, how you care for it makes a big difference. Here's what we tell our customers.

Leave the soil plugs

Let them break down on the lawn and fall back into the holes. If you seeded, the seed mixes in with the loosened soil. Your mower will often break the plugs up within two to three weeks.

Keep it moist for 3 weeks

Water the seed in right away. For the first three weeks keep the surface damp, not soaking wet. You'll start to see grass sprouting from the holes. For the next three weeks, water more thoroughly but less often. Early morning is best.

Limit traffic

Keep foot traffic to a minimum, pets included. Staying off the lawn for the first few weeks gives the seed a better chance to take.

Mow carefully

Mowing weekly is fine once the seed is well watered in and the grass is about 4 inches tall. Use a sharp blade for a clean cut that won't pull up new seedlings, and take off no more than one third of the blade height each time. Leave the clippings. They can hold some seed.

Go easy with fall leaves

Seedlings are delicate with shallow roots. Raking tears them up. Lightly blow the leaves off, or mulch them with the mower.

Common questions

How soon can I use my new lawn?

It depends on seed or sod and the weather. We'll go over it before we start.

When should I seed?

Early fall is often a good window. Spring can work too. We'll recommend timing for your yard.

Can you fix just part of my lawn?

Yes. Patching bare areas is a common job.

Does the ground need work first?

Often it does. See drainage and grading.

When is the best time to aerate?

Spring and especially fall. We recommend aerating at least once a year, and fall is the ideal time to combine it with overseeding.

Do I need to pick up the soil plugs?

No. Leave them. They break down over the next week or two and work back into the soil.
